# The seven ports

Ask the questions in the order given, one per turn. Push back once on a vague answer before moving on. Items marked **required** cannot be skipped; everything else can, and a skip goes under Gaps to overcome.

## Contents

- Port 1. Harbour of Setting Out
- Port 2. Isle of Charts
- Port 3. Shipwright's Cove
- Port 4. The Reef
- Port 5. Quartermaster's Reach
- Port 6. Red Team Rock
- Port 7. The Lighthouse

Each port ends with a treasure. Name it when the port's result is recorded, in one line.

## Port 1. Harbour of Setting Out

**Purpose:** an honest reading of where the product stands, then the challenge in the founder's own words.

1. **Where you stand.** Ask the founder to rate seven areas as strong, partial, weak or unknown. Ask all seven in one turn, since this is one rating exercise, and accept a short reply such as "partial, weak, strong, ?, weak, unknown, partial". If the founder answers something else instead, record that answer and ask for the ratings once more before treating them as skipped.
   - The problem: is it one someone already pays to solve?
   - The segment: who exactly, and is it growing?
   - Value differentiation: what do you know or have that others do not?
   - AI and data: does the AI do work that rules or people could not, and do you own the data it needs?
   - Delivery: can someone other than you deliver it?
   - Economics: what does one unit of work cost to serve?
   - Risk and trust: what happens when it is wrong, and who carries that?
   Any of the seven may be "unknown". An "unknown" or a skip on the problem, the segment or the economics goes under Gaps to overcome; do not ask again.
2. **The challenge (required).** What is actually stopping you? In your own words. If it does not fit a shape, do not force it into one. Accept it only when it is at least one full sentence and says what blocks the founder.
3. **The cost of inaction.** What does it cost to leave it as it is? Six months from now, unchanged: in the metric, in money, or in what you cannot do.
4. **Why now.** What makes this quarter the moment: a buying cycle, a raise, a rule coming into force, a competitor, the runway?
5. **Three framings.** Two other ways of saying the challenge, if they have them. Choose by strategic weight, not by the lowest score. Settle on one within three days; it is an iteration, not a commitment.
6. **Failure risks.** Which do you recognise? Offer these four as prompts, not a complete list:
   - Tech-push: building first, finding the buyer later.
   - The pilot catch-22, a loop: no pilots, so no real data, so no credible evaluation, so no buyer trust, so no pilots.
   - Thin capacity: a solo founder or one developer carrying a roadmap sized for a team.
   - Feature roadmaps: timelines of things to build with no evidence gates between them.
   If the founder names another, record it in their words.
7. **The baseline.** One number that is true today: the metric, the figure and its label. A baseline of zero is still a baseline. Never a market size.

**Treasure:** The Baseline Compass. The challenge in the founder's own words, with what blocks it, and the baseline.

## Port 2. Isle of Charts

**Purpose:** choose the roadmap that answers the challenge.

1. **Build or bring.** Are you building a roadmap, or bringing one you already have? If they bring one, lay it out row by row and ask, for each row: keep, change or drop.
2. **The type (required).** Strategic, initiative or feature. Explain the three in one line each using `roadmaps.md`, then ask which answers the challenge from Port 1. If the founder offers a feature list and calls it strategy, name the mismatch before anything else.
3. **Why that one.** How does it connect to the challenge? If they cannot connect them, they have picked the document, not the tool. Say so.
4. **What it lets you learn.** A roadmap is an instrument to validate and learn. They share it to get the challenge back.
5. **Reader and review.** Who reads it first, and when will it be reviewed? Early on it can change within a month; during pilots it settles to the quarter. Ask for the next review date.

**Treasure:** The Chart Case. A roadmap chosen for a reason, with a review date.

## Port 3. Shipwright's Cove

**Purpose:** build the parts the chosen roadmap needs, and nothing it does not. Use the order for the type chosen in Port 2. Templates are in `roadmaps.md`.

**Strategic roadmap**

1. The big statement: one sentence about the growth ahead that a board member could repeat without the page.
2. Today to target: the same metric at both ends, today's figure (from Port 1) and the target with its year.
3. **Growth levers (at least one required):** three to five levers, not features. For each: the growth lever type (market, product, channel, platform or partnership) and the outcome. Ask for volume, price and months live so the contribution can be computed; anything missing is `[placeholder]`.
4. One line per lever: the opportunity, and what the founder will do to capture it.
5. The timeline, year by year. Enablers and compliance before what depends on them.
6. Signals to adapt: what would make them re-sequence, scale or drop a lever. The threshold is written now, before any result.
7. Runway: can the runway carry this, what is gated on a raise, and where investors, customers and partners come in.

Then show the bridge: baseline plus each lever's contribution against the target. If it falls short, say so and ask which assumption changes or which target moves.

**Initiative roadmap**

1. The strategic aim these quarters add up to.
2. Goals with measurable targets: the goal, the measure and number, by when.
3. Time allocation before the quarter starts: innovation, data and evaluation, trust and compliance, maintenance. The founder chooses the split.
4. The real team: roles and hours a week. The first focus: the one segment, market or product they start with, and why.
5. **Initiatives by quarter (at least one required):** each with an outcome, an owner, the evidence of progress, and a gate (measure, threshold, decision date, and what happens on go, change or stop).
6. The first initiative specified for thirty days: owner, value to the aim, cost, the evidence that exists by day 30, and the decision it informs.
7. The stop list: what they will not do this quarter. On the radar: known about, not committed to.
8. The market clock: dates set by someone else (a rule coming into force, a partner release, a buying cycle, the runway) and how the sequence anchors to them.

**Feature roadmap**

1. The goal these features serve.
2. **Features by month (at least one required)**, each tied to the goal it moves.
3. Dependencies: what each one waits on.
4. Remind the founder once that this is an internal document, and that the strategic or initiative layer is what a board, buyer or investor will ask for.

**Treasure:** The Shipwright's Square. The parts the roadmap needs, and none it does not.

## Port 4. The Reef

**Purpose:** seven things no AI roadmap should leave aside.

1. Ask first: is this an AI product, where the product depends on work a model does? If not, record "not an AI product" and pass the Reef without questions.
2. Otherwise work through the seven integrations in `ai-gates.md`, one at a time. They are in English in the file; ask them in the founder's language. For each, ask for the output and whether the gate is met. If there is no model yet, record that honestly instead of a claim.

**Treasure:** The Diver's Lamp. The AI gates assessed, or the honest note that there is no model to gate.

## Port 5. Quartermaster's Reach

**Purpose:** five columns, one horizon, ranked.

1. **The horizon (required):** twelve or twenty-four months, for every row. The same initiative can return very different figures at twelve and at twenty-four months, so mixed horizons make a roadmap meaningless.
2. For each row from Port 3, ask for:
   - Value added: money per month that can be attributed to it, with its label.
   - Months live: how many months of the horizon it is live and producing that value.
   - Build cost: one-off.
   - Run cost: per month, including AI usage, hosting, human review and model upkeep.
   - Teams involved.
3. Calculate, and show the working. Each result starts with `Calculated:`.
   - Total cost = build + run × months in the horizon
   - Payback (months) = build ÷ (monthly value − monthly run)
   - Total value = monthly value × months live within the horizon
   - Return = (total value − total cost) ÷ total cost
   If monthly value does not exceed monthly run, say the row never pays back on these figures.
4. Rank the rows by return, then ask whether the order matches what the founder would actually do first, and why not if it does not.

**Treasure:** The Quartermaster's Ledger. Five columns, one horizon, ranked.

## Port 6. Red Team Rock

**Purpose:** the roadmap under stress. Details are in `boardings.md`.

1. **The four boardings.** The customer, the buyer, the regulator and the investor, one at a time. Each asks their hardest question about this roadmap. End each with a rehearsal verdict: held, honest, weak or silent, with the reason. For each question not answered with evidence, ask: a risk on the roadmap, a change in sequence, or a cut?
2. **The six building blocks.** For each, the roadmap question first, then the product question. A product question without evidence becomes a stated risk, a dependency or an early initiative. Never a hidden assumption.
3. **Two disruptions.** A six-month funding delay, and an assurance delay. For each: what becomes exposed, the one roadmap change that answers it, and the evidence needed to confirm it. The founder may swap one for a market event from the list in `boardings.md`.
4. **Dependencies and crew.** For each initiative: what it waits on, and who is short.

**Treasure:** The Captain's Scar. Four boardings faced, six blocks under stress.

## Port 7. The Lighthouse

**Purpose:** the audit and the plan to communicate. Details are in `lighthouse.md`.

1. Run the audit checklist for the roadmap's type. Mark each item yes, partly or no.
2. Name the three fixes with the most impact, and ask whether the founder wants to fix them now.
3. Who reads it first, in what form, when, and how feedback returns. Offer the 1-1-1 request: one strength, one improvement, one next step.
4. Offer the audience versions: board and investors, buyers, the team.
5. Run the confidentiality check before anything leaves the founding team.
6. Produce the roadmap in the output format.

**Treasure:** The Keeper's Lens. The audit read, and the plan to communicate the roadmap written.
