## The audit

Mark each item yes, partly or no. Use the list for the roadmap's type, plus the AI list if the Reef was not passed as "not an AI product".

**Strategic roadmap**

- One big statement of the growth ahead.
- Today's baseline and the target, in one business metric.
- Three to five initiatives, each adding a stated value to the target.
- The bridge shown: the contributions add up to the target, or the gap is named.
- Value, cost, teams, payback and return per initiative, on one horizon.
- Every number labelled. Money: signed, forecast, pipeline, market size or target. Operating figures: measured or estimate.
- Signals that would make the founder re-sequence, scale or drop an initiative.

**Initiative roadmap**

- Each initiative linked to a strategic aim with a business outcome.
- Goals with measurable targets.
- Enablers and compliance sequenced first.
- A gate for each step.
- The first initiative specified: owner, value, cost, 30-day evidence.
- One first focus, a stop list, nothing without an owner.

**Feature roadmap**

- Stamped internal.
- Every feature tied to a goal.
- Dependencies stated.
- The initiative layer above it named.

**AI**

- The AI passes the "remove the AI" test for one buyer.
- A kept-aside test set, a baseline and a first measured result exist, or the claim is marked `[not yet measured]`.
- The next pilot is designed to produce labelled real data.
- Cost to serve includes AI usage and human-review time.
- There is an answer to "the platform ships it" and to a model change.
- Assurance is scheduled before scale.

## The three fixes

Name the three no or partly items with the most impact on the meeting ahead. Ask whether the founder wants to fix them now. Then stop polishing: a roadmap is a space to test and learn, and three fixes is the limit of what anyone acts on this week.

## Feedback with the 1-1-1 rule

Offer this request for the founder to send to the first readers:

```text
What works: one key strength.
What to improve: one key improvement.
What next: one recommended next step.
```

Then ask: who reads it first, in what form, when, and how does feedback come back?

## Audience versions

One internal roadmap, lighter versions for each room. Same bet, different emphasis. Offer them; write only the ones asked for.

| Audience | What they want |
|---|---|
| Board and investors | The target, the milestones, the return |
| Buyers | Value, risk, return, assurance |
| The team | Goals, owners, gates |

Show direction and outcomes, not feature dates. Keep pipeline separate from signed revenue in every version.

## Confidentiality check

Before anything leaves the founding team. Each change below is a proposal; show the founder the before and after, and apply it only when they accept:

- Personal data removed, or a lawful basis and a retention period set.
- Client names replaced with client types, unless the client has agreed.
- Nothing a client, partner or employee said in confidence.
- Pipeline shown separately from signed revenue.
- Ranges or totals where an exact figure would identify a deal.
- No dates that have already passed.
- Superseded versions retired, so old numbers stop circulating.
- This version matches its audience.

Planning support, not legal advice.
